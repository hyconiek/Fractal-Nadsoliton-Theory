## Abstract

Release 10.62 executes FIN programs ST94–ST105 and continues the falsification of the counterfactual hypothesis that observable reality is an operational projection or quotient of a deeper FIN realization.

ST94 proves a relative uniqueness theorem: after a unital observable algebra and normalized trace are supplied, the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto that algebra is unique. The theorem does not derive the observable algebra, accessible sector, or apparatus from strict FIN.

ST95 classifies the complete real symmetric graph-Laplacian fiber over the twelve-state strict operator. Every 24-state lift satisfying `LP=PA12` has the unique block form

```text
L = P A12 P* + F B F*.
```

The admissible fine block `B` ranges over a 78-dimensional convex spectrahedral set. Positivity requires `B >= 0`; graph-sign constraints require `B_ii >= A_ii` and `A_ij <= B_ij <= -A_ij`. The former one-parameter dyadic `q` family is only a small subfamily. Thus the exact nonidentifiability found in ST86 is substantially larger than previously established.

ST96 derives the exact Petz-recovery frontier. Coarse/fine pinching loses precisely the cross-sector coherence, with relative-entropy loss `D(rho || Pi rho)` and exact recovery iff `rho = Pi rho`. ST97 constructs a state-dependent twelve-branch selector but proves that the symmetric state remains nonselecting; initial phase relocates rather than removes the selector premise. ST98 extends the passive-memory obstruction to reciprocal positive-real state-space realizations.

ST99 interval-certifies the first nonlinear fold for the frozen binary64 strict operator. A 15-variable Krawczyk calculation proves a unique root in a radius-`1e-8` box, with strict inclusion margin approximately `9.9999871e-9`. This is not yet a transcendental-parameter enclosure of the kernel.

ST100 proves that degree-two refinement squares `Z2` holonomy, so an antiperiodic spin structure becomes periodic on the next level. Maintaining antiperiodicity requires a new twist at every refinement. ST101 proves that one deliberately fine/global scalar identifies the dyadic hidden parameter: `B(q)=B(0)+2qI`, and the globally normalized Gibbs coarse-sector probability is strictly monotone in `q`. ST102 constructs a commuting conditional-expectation tower.

ST103 proves the robust common-contamination endpoint by data processing but does not interval-certify the independent two-parameter nuisance rectangle. ST104 proves `TO ⊆ CGP ⊊ GP`, leaving the first strictness question open without a declared bath/dilation class. ST105 proves that conditioned coarse records cannot distinguish hidden alternatives, while a supplied coarse/fine occupation measurement can distinguish them in a fully specified synthetic protocol.

The central conclusion is that FIN now has a mathematically sharp theory of finite spectral shadows, hidden lift fibers, recoverability, and conditional falsification. It still does not select the physical observable algebra or connect the projection to experimental reality.
