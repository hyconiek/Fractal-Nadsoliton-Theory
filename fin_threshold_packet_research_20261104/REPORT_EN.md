# Finite-energy threshold packets disperse on the critical width-time scale

TP constructs exactly continuous, even finite-energy packets using the
normalized Darboux map of the supplied operator. It proves a local
threshold expansion with explicit Gaussian envelope and controlled field
and velocity remainders through time t=O(R^2), where R is packet width.
The potential and bound projection are retained exactly.

The envelope is proportional to (1+i tau/m)^(-1/2), tau=t/R^2. A global
autocorrelation calculation shows that freezing it has a nonvanishing
error at fixed nonzero tau. Therefore a packet of width epsilon^-1 cannot
be treated as a fixed threshold carrier across time epsilon^-2 merely
by taking epsilon smaller.

For this linear comparison family, the integrated squared compact trace
is proportional to R. At amplitude epsilon and width epsilon^-1 it is
of order epsilon, not epsilon^2. This is a constraint on what generic
norm/parity/projection bounds can prove, not a counterexample for the
original nonlinear preparation with zero initial continuum radiation.

The local quadratic source expansion supplies explicit slowly varying
coefficients for DT's static and outgoing harmonic profiles. Its error
has only a negative spatial weight, so a retarded cascade estimate still
requires additional control of the spatial tails. No whole nonlinear
cubic trace or damping theorem is claimed.
