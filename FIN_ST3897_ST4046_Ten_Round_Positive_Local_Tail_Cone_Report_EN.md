# FIN ST3897–ST4046 — Ten-Round Positive Local Tail Cone

## Cone theorem

Fix the six strict conductances and let \(t_d\ge0\), \(d\ge7\), be any tail
with finite second moment. Then

\[
\lambda(k)=2\sum_{d\ge1}w_d(1-\cos kd)
=Ck^2+o(k^2),

\]

where

\[
C=C_0+\sum_{d\ge7}d^2t_d,
\qquad
C_0=\sum_{d=1}^6d^2W_d=3.8153141154998296.

\]

Every positive finite-second-moment continuation is therefore locally
quadratic, but its coefficient is not fixed.

## Exact coefficient and speed range

Nonnegativity implies \(C\ge C_0\), with equality only for the zero tail. Every
target \(C_*\ge C_0\) is attained by the one-atom tail

\[
t_7=\frac{C_*-C_0}{49}.

\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{C\in[C_0,\infty)}

\]

and the dimensionless wave-speed proxy satisfies

\[
\boxed{widehat c\in[1.9532829071846785,\infty)}.

\]

All members have the same one-dimensional local spectral exponent, while their
propagation coefficients vary without upper bound.

## Constraint tests

Fixing total tail mass \(M\) yields only

\[
C-C_0\ge49M;

\]

the upper bound remains infinite because mass may move to arbitrarily large
distance. Even an upper mass budget leaves the coefficient unbounded. A finite
support cutoff gives bounds but introduces the cutoff \(R\) and all tail
weights as additional data.

## Conditional minimum principle

Minimizing \(C\), or any strictly positive tail norm, uniquely selects
\(t=0\), hence the truncated continuation and \(\widehat c_{min}\). This is a
valid conditional variational theorem. The repository does not provide a
reason why nature should minimize tail second moment or propagation speed.

Smooth positive exponential tails

\[
t_d=Ae^{-a(d-6)}

\]

retain quadratic locality but leave \(A\) and \(a\) free. Smoothness therefore
does not restore uniqueness.

## Physical boundary

The speed proxy is dimensionless. A physical velocity would be

\[
c_{\rm phys}=\frac{\ell_*}{\tau_*}\widehat c.

\]

Neither \(\ell_*\) nor \(\tau_*\) is strict-derived. The lower bound is only a
bound inside the chosen positive-local continuation class, not a bound in
metres per second.
