## 1. Equilibrium weights

The three sectors carry nearly all equilibrium mass and are exactly
symmetry-equivalent.

    N=4: sector mass=0.320824; k6 mass=0.000944555; P(other sector before k6)=0.978333; mean hit=14.385502; cap/pi=0.147539

    N=6: sector mass=0.330832; k6 mass=0.000134983; P(other sector before k6)=0.987652; mean hit=43.740192; cap/pi=0.062608

By contrast the combined ±k6 basin mass is tiny in this small-N range.

## 2. Competing-target committor

Starting from equilibrium conditioned on C0, solve the exact committor:

    hit C1 or C2 before either k6 basin?

The answer is between

    97.8% and 99.6%

for N=3..6.

So the exact process overwhelmingly moves among localized sectors before
visiting the parity-bit basins.

This is the opposite of what would be required for k6 to define the dominant
coarse unit at these N.

## 3. Capacity trend

The exact capacity-derived scale

    cap(C0,C1 union C2)/pi(C0)

decreases from about

    0.254 at N=3

to

    0.0626 at N=6.

The mean first-hitting time of another localized sector increases from

    7.28

to

    43.74

in the same microscopic clock units.

This is already a genuine slow coarse process generated by the exact
microscopic chain.

## 4. Boundary

The hard basin process is not exactly Markov and the raw capacity ratio is not
by itself the final three-state rate.

Transition-state and hidden-state residence create memory.

Reports 141-142 derive the memory-renormalized slow generator instead of
assigning rates directly from barriers or capacities.
