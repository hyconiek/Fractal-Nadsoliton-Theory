# FIXED-VALENCE-CANDIDATE-160
## A degree constraint creates sparsity but merely moves the missing law into a new integer parameter

Date: 2026-09-26

Status:
structural candidate/no-go boundary.

One direct way to avoid the dense-graph theorem is to impose

    degree(x)=d

for every unit.

Then a maximum-entropy relation ensemble over all admissible graphs is a
random d-regular graph.

This has finite degree as n grows without an n-dependent edge chemical
potential.

So fixed valence is a genuine mathematical escape from report 158.

## But the physical problem is not solved

The integer

    d

is now a new dimensionless structural parameter.

Nothing in the current single-cell FIN core selects:
- d=2;
- d=3;
- d=4;
- or any other value.

Different d produce different:
- spectra;
- expansion/mixing properties;
- effective resistance;
- collective transition thresholds.

Moreover a random regular graph supplies graph-theoretic sparsity but not
Euclidean locality or a spatial dimension.

For d>=3 it is generically locally tree-like rather than lattice-like.

## Conclusion

A valence law is a plausible TYPE of missing principle.

But imposing a valence by hand is not a derivation of incidence.

A successful FIN composition theory would need to derive the valence rule
from a deeper conserved/relational quantity.
