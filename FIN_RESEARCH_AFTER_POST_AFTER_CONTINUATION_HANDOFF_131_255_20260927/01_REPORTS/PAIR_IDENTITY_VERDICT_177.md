# PAIR-IDENTITY-VERDICT-177
## Persistence can be obtained by conservation, but incidence still lacks a derived selector

Date: 2026-09-26

Reports 175-176 close the strongest simple persistence route.

### Dynamic bonds

Can select/change relations, but natural common-clock dynamics rewires them too
quickly.

### Exactly conserved bonds

Persist forever, but cannot choose their own graph sector.

### Quenched random selection

Creates a persistent sparse graph, but physical predictions depend on the
realized graph unless a new universality theorem removes that dependence.

## Updated bottleneck

A complete multicell FIN law must provide THREE layers:

1. `support law`
   which pairs may be active;

2. `selection law`
   which sparse support is realized;

3. `persistence law`
   why the selected support survives over the effective-node dynamics.

The previous campaigns have shown that none of these three is automatically
supplied by:
- exchangeability;
- fixed edge count;
- fixed valence;
- node phases;
- holonomy receivers;
- exact conservation alone.

## Next atom

`RELATIONAL-SELECTION-BY-METASTABILITY-178`

The remaining constructive route is a two-stage process:

    symmetric relational dynamics
      ->
    spontaneous selection of one sparse graph sector
      ->
    exponentially slow graph switching.

Test whether a fixed, n-independent relational action can produce BOTH:
- a sparse selected graph;
- a barrier growing with the appropriate system size.

Report 172 already blocks the simplest edge+triangle action, so 178 should
search only structurally distinct mechanisms, not reparameterizations of the
same dense model.
