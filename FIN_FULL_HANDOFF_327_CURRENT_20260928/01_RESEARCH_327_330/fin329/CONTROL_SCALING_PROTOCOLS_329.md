# FIN 329 — CONTROL-SCALING-PROTOCOLS
## Fixed kappa, fixed defect field, and fixed information budget are different preparation experiments

Date: 2026-09-28

Status: **EXACT identity + finite-N protocol comparison; no physical protocol selected.**

## 1. Exact control parameter

From the exact defect representation,

`mu_{N,kappa}/mu_{N,0} proportional to exp[-theta D]`,

with

`theta = kappa/N`.

Therefore `theta`, not `kappa` itself, is the direct field conjugate to the defect count `D`.

This makes three commonly discussed protocols inequivalent:

1. **fixed kappa**: `theta=kappa/N` weakens with N;
2. **fixed theta**: `kappa=N theta`, constant defect field per defect;
3. **fixed information cost**: choose `kappa_N` so that `D_KL(mu_{N,kappa_N} || mu_{N,0})` is constant.

No one of these is selected by FIN merely from the statistical family. The intended preparation experiment must choose the control convention.

## 2. Exact KL formula

For the pinning family,

`D_KL(mu_kappa || mu_0)`

` = kappa E_kappa[p0] - log E_0[exp(kappa p0)]`.

Equivalently, in defect coordinates the constant `exp(kappa)` cancels and the family is generated by `exp[-theta D]`.

## 3. Frozen comparison anchor

Use the already studied point

`N=6, kappa=12`, hence `theta=2`.

At this anchor:

- `KL = 0.2689473196` nats = **0.3880089643 bits**;
- `E[D] = 0.04221843`.

Thus fixed `kappa=12` visibly weakens the preparation as N grows. Fixed `theta=2` keeps both defect count and information cost much more stable over this range. The fixed-KL protocol also requires approximately `kappa proportional to N` here, with `theta` settling near 2.2–2.3 by N=8..10.

## 5. Why this differs from a naive sqrt(N) expectation

A `sqrt(N)` control scaling is natural in a regular central-limit regime where the controlled extensive observable has variance proportional to N and the imposed change remains in the Gaussian bulk.

The current localized finite-N regime is different: the mean-field localized minimum has

`p0* ~= 0.98069235`,

so the rough mean-field scale `N(1-p0*)` is only about 0.23 at N=12. The presently studied systems are therefore still in a sparse-defect / boundary-layer regime rather than a fully developed eleven-dimensional Gaussian cloud.

This does **not** rule out a later crossover toward Gaussian/sqrt(N) behavior. It says only that imposing that asymptotic scaling on N<=12 is not supported by the exact finite-N preparation statistics.

## 6. Decision consequence

The earlier observation that preparation influence diminishes with N at fixed `kappa` cannot be interpreted automatically as a universal FIN prediction. Part of it is simply the experimental convention `theta=kappa/N -> 0`.

A future held-out test must freeze the control protocol before looking at the new data. A reasonable candidate for the current few-defect regime is fixed `theta`, because it is the direct conjugate field in the exact defect law, but selecting it as a physical law would require an operational principle or experiment.
