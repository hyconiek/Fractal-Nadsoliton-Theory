## Abstract

Release 11.06 reports six adaptive research rounds, ST1992–ST2081, classifying
positive face-curvature energies under exact FIN refinement.

For 12-to-24 refinement, coarse curvature embeds as two identical horizontal
copies and zero product-square curvature. Energy compatibility requires

\[
\kappa_{f,0}+\kappa_{f,1}=\kappa_f.
\]

Fiber-swap symmetry uniquely gives \(\kappa_f/2\) on each inherited copy, but
all 66 new square weights remain arbitrary. After D12 reduction they form an
\((\mathbb R_+)^6\) cone.

At 24-to-48, base faces carry quarter weights, old squares split into halves,
and 144 new squares appear. The symmetric vertical measure cone has dimension
at least 13 after two steps and grows under every further nontrivial
refinement. Exact GF(2) boundary ranks show H1 remains zero for the chosen
product complexes, so the freedom is dynamical rather than a failure of
topological closure.

Stronger naturality and homogeneity do not select a measure. Separate
bilinearity and associativity force only

\[
\mu=cwq,
\]

leaving the constant, base face set, base measure, gauge coupling and scales
free.

The exact Schur/minimal-extension calculation gives

\[
E(a,r,s)=\frac{k}{2}(a^2+r^2)+\frac{\mu}{2}(2r+s)^2,
\]

\[
r=s=0,qquad E_{\min}(a)=\frac{k}{2}a^2.
\]

Thus coarse classical energy is completely independent of the vertical Hodge
weight. No coarse energy identity or Schur complement can identify vertical
face weights. Fine holonomy data or a quantum/fluctuation measure is required.

A tensor-product Hodge inner product supplies a coherent positive conditional
rule proportional to \(wq\), but inherits the unsolved base complex, base
measure, factor normalization, q, differential calculus and action scale.

The highest-value next theorem is to classify all D12-equivariant second
differentials \(d_1:C^1\to C^2\) satisfying \(d_1d_0=0\) and the
12-to-24-to-48 refinement intertwining equations. A one-dimensional normalized
solution would be a breakthrough; higher dimension would be a decisive no-go
for unique gauge geometry from current FIN data.
