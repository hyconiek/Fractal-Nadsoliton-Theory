### RG-C02: generated three-body term and controlled static truncation

For four labelled copies, integrating out the fourth gives, up to a constant,

`F3(n)=g*n^T*A7*n/8 + log(sum_j exp[g*(A7*n)_j/4])`.

The canonical uniform-reference ANOVA decomposition is an exact identity.
Its third component cannot be removed by a change of pairwise coefficients
in the tested model. With plus sign at `(0,0,0)` and label alternatives
`{0,1}`, `{0,2}`, `{0,4}`, the independently enclosed alternating contrast is

`0.271270584588549 <= Delta3 <= 0.271270584588550`.

Every purely constant/one-body/pairwise log-weight has zero such contrast.
This accepts a scoped **validated non-pairwise-closure witness** for the
exact-decimal model.

For the canonical pair truncation, the independently enclosed residual
oscillation satisfies

`0.205388295447560 <= omega <= 0.205388295447805`.

The finite-alphabet reweighting theorem gives

`TV(P_F,P_pair) <= tanh(omega/4) <= 0.051301995505`.

To see the sharp universal bound, set the raw likelihood range to
`[1,R]`, `R=exp(omega)`. For a fixed mean `m`, convexity bounds the absolute
deviation by the two-endpoint distribution. Its TV is at most
`(R-m)*(m-1)/(m*(R-1))`; maximizing over `1<=m<=R` gives
`(sqrt(R)-1)/(sqrt(R)+1)`. Constant shifts therefore cost no TV.
Fixed observation channels contract this error.

The directly enumerated actual pair-truncation TV is approximately
**0.023358855088919**. It is a numerical value, distinct from the validated
upper bound. Direct and sequential marginalization agree numerically in the
declared four-copy model. A three-coordinate third-order-inclusive ANOVA
reconstruction is exact by construction; it is not evidence of compact
closure at arbitrarily many scales.

The source certificate's extremely long printed decimal upper endpoints
were serialized with nearest rounding. The intake retains conservative,
explicitly outward-rounded public endpoints above; do not infer that every
last printed source digit is an outward endpoint.

### RG-C03: nonlumpability and equilibrium memory

The three-copy leave-one-out chain has 1,728 labelled states. Retaining the
first two ordered labels gives 144 observed states. The microstates
`(0,0,0)` and `(0,0,1)` have the same observed state, but their rates to
observed `(1,0)` differ. The exact-decimal difference is enclosed by

`0.171914174447984 <= rate_difference <= 0.171914174447985`.

The observed partition is therefore **not strongly lumpable**. This does
not state that every conceivable preparation-specific closure is impossible.

The stationary conditional average of the original generator and a newly
declared Gibbs sampler of the marginal both preserve the marginal stationary
distribution, but they have different rates and finite-time predictions.
For the equilibrium hidden preparation given observed `(0,0)`, reproduced
TV discrepancies between the actual projection and the averaged generator are:

| Generator time t | TV discrepancy |
|---:|---:|
| 0.15 | 0.001532323470409 |
| 0.5 | 0.011884158949148 |
| 1.0 | 0.030431010465497 |
| 1.5 | 0.046903007606487 |

These are numerical finite-time diagnostics, not a uniform error theorem.
At t=0.5, the preparations with hidden label0 and hidden label1 differ by
approximately **0.357778158774433 TV** despite identical visible labels.
The preparation dependence is therefore material.

For the unnormalized equilibrium scalar channel
`f=1{observed=(0,0)}` and `v=QLPf`, the inspected source computes

`K(0)=<v,v>_pi ~= 0.009952015225620`,
`K'(0)=<v,Lv>_pi ~= -0.018039981780484`.

The source fields `M0_coupled` and `M1_derivative` name these quantities.
They are **not** the integrated moments
`M0=int K(t)dt`, `M1=int t*K(t)dt` used by earlier FIN memory reports.
Those moments and a certified memory-tail bound were not computed here.
The saved K(t) values are numerical evaluations of the declared reversible
quadratic channel. Hidden-preparation dependence has its own inhomogeneous
term and cannot be inferred from stationary K(t) alone.

### RG-C04: the mechanism is not unique to FIN

Trace-matched full-Potts and flat-P7 controls show nonzero generated
three-body contrasts and nonlumpability/memory too. Their reported contrasts
are approximately `0.165094893090473` and `0.274095210357638`, respectively.
These finite control calculations were reproduced numerically. They were
not given the FIN exact-kernel interval certification.

Thus the **existence** of generated many-body terms and memory does not
select A7. Kernel-dependent coefficients remain legitimate finite-model
contrasts; three models do not establish a universality theorem. The
countermodel normalization is trace-matched at the same gain.

### RG-C05: accept static consistency, keep the process test open

The event `sigma1=sigma3` has a pair-truncation prediction about
`0.761967899024763` and direct parent probability about
`0.7794674990932`; the discrepancy `0.0174996000684` is below the static
bound. This verifies a consequence of the finite static error budget.

It does **not** fulfill the plan's finite-window dynamic prediction task.
The producer had already enumerated the entire exact retained equilibrium
law before writing the event contract. Writing that contract before a
second enumeration of the same law is not strong evidence of a blind
holdout. Accept **STATIC_COMPUTATIONAL_CONSISTENCY**, not empirical
validation or a completed independent process prediction.
