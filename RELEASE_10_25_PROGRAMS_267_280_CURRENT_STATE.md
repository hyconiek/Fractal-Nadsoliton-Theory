## Abstract

This release reports fourteen executed mathematical and computational research
programs, P267–P280, and gives a consolidated account of the present FIN
theory.

The strict FIN operator is treated as a finite positive self-adjoint generator
whose spectral calculus produces unitary wave evolution, diffusive evolution,
Green/resolvent families, and context-dependent Schur memory. The legacy kernel
is retained only as an intermediate bridge object and is not silently
substituted for the strict kernel.

The release proves uniqueness of the visible atomic Stieltjes measure while
showing that its numerical inverse is ill-conditioned; gives an executable
categorical core for Schur reductions; constructs a minimal six-outcome POVM
for five independent current observables; proves a rank-defect no-go theorem
for simple linear size-restoring RG liftings; constructs a conditional quantum
process-information ledger; and tests one positive-shift legacy completion
atom, which repairs positivity but does not reproduce the strict spectral or
memory shape.

Further programs certify the absence of an admissible external P241 laboratory
bundle, optimize a synthetic two-flux tomography design, derive deterministic
spectral-fingerprint exclusion radii, prove a calibrated two-time semigroup
consistency theorem, identify an adaptive law within a frozen intervention
panel, and benchmark FIN as a nonlinear reservoir against matched controls.

Finally, the release derives the exact Gibbs relative-entropy/free-energy
identity and constructs a product torsor for scale and orientation. These two
results clarify the present physical boundary: dimensional energy and a
directed orientation can be supplied consistently, but neither is generated by
the current strict FIN object.

The overall conclusion is that FIN is presently a rigorous finite spectral and
informational model with explicit operational interfaces, not yet a completed
physical theory. Fourteen next programs, P281–P294, are ranked by proof value,
falsifiability, and expected scientific return.

## Principal results

1. **Visible Stieltjes identifiability.** Exact visible pole-residue data are
   unique, while noisy inverse recovery is severely ill-conditioned.
2. **Schur category.** Identity, nested composition, and positivity
   preservation hold; 50,000 chains give maximum residual
   \(8.88\times10^{-16}\).
3. **Current POVM.** Five independent current coefficients require at least
   six outcomes, and a regular-simplex construction attains the bound.
4. **RG obstruction.** A lifted \(n\)-state Laplacian has rank at most
   \(n-1\), so no linear isometric lift with \(n<12\) can equal the rank-eleven
   strict generator.
5. **Quantum information ledger.** Record, conditional-state, and discarded
   distinguishability form an exact data-processing balance for a supplied
   quantum instrument.
6. **Legacy completion test.** A positive mean-zero-sector shift repairs the
   generator cone but leaves strict fingerprint defect 0.4865 and normalized
   self-energy defect 0.8680.
7. **Empirical boundary.** No production P241 package exists in the repository;
   P242 is not authorized as a physical confirmatory execution.
8. **Dynamics test.** Calibrated matrix logarithms give an exact two-time
   homogeneous-semigroup criterion.
9. **Adaptive dynamics.** Interventions raise the design rank and recover the
   frozen synthetic law inside a finite panel.
10. **Reservoir benchmark.** FIN exceeds all sixty matched controls on one of
    four tasks, NARMA10, but no general computational advantage is established.
11. **Information-to-energy boundary.** Relative entropy becomes free energy
    only after \(H\), \(T\), and \(k_B\) are supplied.
12. **Independent missing resources.** Positive scale and orientation form an
    \(\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb Z_2\) product torsor; a section supplies both
    but generates neither.
