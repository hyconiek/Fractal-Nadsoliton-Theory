## 1. S12-equivariant one-label generator

Let Q be a continuous-time Markov generator on 12 labels.

Assume full permutation equivariance:

    P Q P^T = Q

for every permutation P in S12.

All ordered off-diagonal pairs (i,j), i!=j, lie in one S12 orbit.

Therefore all off-diagonal rates are equal:

    Q_ij = a,  i!=j.

Row-sum zero then gives

    Q_ii = -11 a.

Writing u=(1/12)1 and gamma=12a,

    boxed:
    Q = gamma(1 u^T-I).

So full S12 symmetry uniquely selects the complete-reset generator up to one
overall clock rate gamma.

On the 11D centered subspace every mode has eigenvalue

    -gamma.

Thus full tangent kinetic isotropy is not an arbitrary extra property if a
genuinely S12-symmetric pre-strict update law exists.

## 2. Stationary state theorem

An irreducible finite Markov generator has a unique stationary distribution.

If Q is S12-equivariant and p is stationary, then for every permutation P,

    Pp

is also stationary.

Uniqueness therefore implies

    Pp=p

for every P in S12.

The only such probability vector is

    p=u=(1/12,...,1/12).

Hence:

    boxed:
    irreducible S12-equivariant Markov dynamics
    cannot have a unique nonuniform stationary distribution.

## 3. Consequence for localized FIN states

The certified localized FIN equilibrium is strongly nonuniform.

Therefore a kinetic law that has that localized state as a unique equilibrium
cannot retain unbroken full S12 permutation symmetry.

At least one of the following must happen:

1. the dynamics is only D12-equivariant after the strict carrier is selected;
2. the state itself spontaneously/selectively breaks S12 and the generator
   depends on that state/order parameter;
3. extra hidden variables make the reduced label process non-Markovian or
   non-irreducible;
4. kinetic symmetry is inherited only approximately or in a pre-localized
   regime.

So a fully symmetric deep update law is compatible with the emergence picture,
but its isotropy is not automatically transferable to the localized strict
regime.
