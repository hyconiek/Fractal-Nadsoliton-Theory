## Lowest dihedral invariant degree controls whether a soft 2D mode generically creates stable daughter phases

## 1. Standard D_m critical representation

Let

    z=x+i y

and let D_m act by

    z -> exp(2 pi i/m) z

and

    z -> conjugate(z).

A real polynomial invariant must be invariant under both operations.

The invariant ring is generated by

    r^2 = z conjugate(z)

and

    Re(z^m).

Therefore the lowest nonradial/angular invariant has degree exactly

    boxed:
    m.

## 2. Immediate consequence

For the standard 2D critical mode:

- D3 allows cubic anisotropy `Re(z^3)`;
- D4 first allows quartic anisotropy `Re(z^4)`;
- D6 first allows sextic anisotropy `Re(z^6)`;
- D12 first allows degree-12 anisotropy.

So a two-dimensional soft mode does not have one universal pitchfork normal
form. Its nonlinear behavior is controlled by the residual stabilizer.

## 3. D3: generic cubic case

The reduced potential is

    V
      = lambda r^2/2
        + c r^3 cos(3phi)
        + beta r^4
        + ...

with c generically nonzero.

The daughter radius is

    r = O(lambda),

not O(sqrt(|lambda|)).

At a daughter stationary point the critical-plane Hessian eigenvalues are

    -lambda
    and
    3 lambda

to leading order.

They have opposite signs.

Therefore a generic D3 cubic crossing does NOT produce a local minimum in the
critical plane.

It produces saddle daughters.

This is exactly what report 66 found for MP7-039.

## 4. D4: quartic selection

For D4 the leading form can be

    V
      = lambda r^2/2
        + r^4[
            beta + c cos(4phi)
          ]
        + higher order.

For lambda<0, nonzero branches satisfy

    r^2
      = -lambda/
        [4(beta+c cos4phi)].

Radial stability requires the denominator to be positive.

Angular minima choose

    cos(4phi) = -sign(c).

Hence stable daughter minima exist generically when

    boxed:
    beta>|c|.

Then four symmetry-related minima are selected.

The opposite four rays are angular saddles.

If beta<=|c|, quartic order is not sufficient to establish bounded stable
daughters and higher orders matter.

## 5. D_m with m>=5

No anisotropic term appears before degree m.

If the radial quartic coefficient is positive,

    V
      = lambda r^2/2
        + beta r^4
        + c r^m cos(mphi)
        + ...

with beta>0.

For lambda<0, the first transition creates an approximately circular ring

    r^2
      ~ -lambda/(4 beta).

The higher-order D_m term then discretizes that ring into m preferred angular
directions.

Thus, unlike D3, stable daughter minima are generically compatible with the
lowest-order local normal form.

## 6. Relevant subgroup hierarchy inside D12

Dihedral stabilizers naturally available as subgroups of D12 include

    D2, D3, D4, D6, D12.

For genuine standard 2D irreducible critical representations:

    D3  -> cubic anisotropy;
    D4  -> quartic anisotropy;
    D6  -> sextic anisotropy;
    D12 -> degree-12 anisotropy.

D2 is exceptional because its real representation theory splits into
one-dimensional characters rather than the same standard irreducible picture.

## 7. FIN interpretation

This gives a concrete symmetry filter for emergent phases:

    softening
      + residual symmetry
      -> lowest allowed nonlinear invariant
      -> daughter topology/stability.

In particular:

    a soft mode alone is insufficient evidence for a new stable phase.

One must know the residual isotropy and the first nonzero invariant coefficient.

For MP7-039 the residual D3 symmetry permits and actually realizes a cubic,
which explains why the new branches are saddles.

## 8. A useful selection principle

Within local Landau-type reductions:

- residual D3 symmetry is generically hostile to immediate stable daughter
  minima because of the cubic invariant;
- D4 or higher dihedral symmetry can protect the critical mode from cubic
  splitting and allows quartic/radial stabilization to create stable daughters.

This is a mathematical selection rule, not a physical law saying nature must
prefer higher stabilizers.
