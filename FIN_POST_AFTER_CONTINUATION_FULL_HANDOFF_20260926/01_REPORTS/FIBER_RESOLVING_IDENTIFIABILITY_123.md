# FIBER-RESOLVING-IDENTIFIABILITY-123
## The fiber rate is invisible to coarse observables but immediately visible to one odd child-resolving observable

Date: 2026-09-26

Status:
- exact identifiability theorem for the ST231 two-child family.

Consider

    L_tilde
      =
      L tensor I_2
      +mu I tensor L_2,

with

    L_2 =
      [[ 1,-1],
       [-1, 1]].

The uniform fiber vector

    u=(1,1)/sqrt(2)

has eigenvalue zero, while the odd fiber vector

    v=(1,-1)/sqrt(2)

has eigenvalue

    2mu.

## Coarse blindness

Every lifted coarse observable has the form

    f tensor u.

For all t,

    exp(-t L_tilde)(f tensor u)
      =
      [exp(-tL)f] tensor u,

independently of mu.

So no experiment restricted to fiber-even/coarse observables can estimate mu.

## One odd observable is enough

Take the child-resolving observable

    sigma=+1 on child +
          =-1 on child -.

Under the stationary uniform fiber law,

    <sigma(t)sigma(0)>
      =
      exp(-2mu t).

Therefore, if dimensionless time t is available,

    boxed:
    mu
      =
      -(1/(2t))
       log <sigma(t)sigma(0)>.

Thus the nonuniqueness is not an abstract mathematical unobservability.

It is exactly a TYPING boundary:

    coarse instruments cannot see mu;

    one fiber-odd instrument can.

## Physical boundary

To turn this into an experimental source of a physical rate one still needs:
- a real child-resolving observable;
- an operational clock;
- evidence that the refined children are physical states rather than a
  mathematical extension.

This locates the missing empirical input very sharply.
