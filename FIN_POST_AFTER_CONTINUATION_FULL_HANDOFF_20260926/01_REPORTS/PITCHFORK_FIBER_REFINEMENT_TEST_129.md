# PITCHFORK-FIBER-REFINEMENT-TEST-129
## The k6 binary split is a real fiber prototype but not yet a recursive refinement law

Date: 2026-09-26

Status:
- structural theorem/no-go separating one successful binary incidence from
  a genuine refinement tower.

The uniform -> ±k6 pitchfork solves two problems at once:

1. child incidence:
       the two daughters are symmetry-related stable minima;

2. fiber rate:
       the parent saddle gives a FIN potential barrier and therefore a
       metastable switching exponent.

This is much stronger than inserting an arbitrary ST231 fiber.

However it is NOT yet the desired recursive refinement.

## 1. Wrong base type for the current 12-minimum geometry

The pitchfork refines ONE uniform parent into TWO parity states.

The metastable geometry of reports 110-116 instead has TWELVE localized parent
states.

No theorem currently says that each of those twelve parents contains an
isomorphic k6-like internal pitchfork.

Therefore one cannot simply tensor the twelve-minimum graph with this binary
split.

## 2. The later symmetry cascade is not a stable-child tower

The next known links

    D6 -> D3
and
    D3 -> Z2

do increase orbit multiplicity

    2 -> 4 -> 12,

but their local daughters are saddles in the relevant parameter direction.

They are branch genealogy, not repeated stable fiber splitting.

So the attractive multiplicity cascade

    1 -> 2 -> 4 -> 12

is NOT a recursive tree of metastable children.

## 3. Parameter dependence

The binary barrier is

    Delta Phi_6(g),

hence the induced fiber rate depends on:
- g;
- N;
- the heat-bath clock/prefactor.

A geometric refinement law would need a rule explaining how those quantities
transform from parent level to child level.

That rule is absent.

## 4. Verdict

The k6 pitchfork provides a successful PROTOTYPE of endogenous binary
incidence.

It does not yet provide:
- a fiber over every coarse state;
- a self-similar iteration;
- a scale ratio;
- physical length.

So it narrows the incidence-law problem but does not close ST231/ST245.
