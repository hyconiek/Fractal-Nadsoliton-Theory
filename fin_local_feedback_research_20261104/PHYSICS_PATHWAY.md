# A proposed route from the current model toward empirical physics

Date: 2026-11-04. Research strategy, not a theorem or a funded experimental
plan. No experiment, acquisition or external communication is initiated.

## Current position

The present chain studies a supplied classical 1+1-dimensional action,
its special invariant sector and prescribed small initial disturbance.
It is useful for learning how localized modes exchange energy with a
continuum. Its mathematical success does not select the action from FIN,
identify its fields with measured particles, or derive quantum theory,
three-dimensional space, electromagnetism or gravity. The strict FIN
objects and the legacy field model still require an explicit bridge.

The strongest near-term target is one controlled transport/damping law
with a stated error and an observable definition. Additional decimal
precision in a frozen coefficient is not a substitute for that target.

## Proposed sequence and evidence required

| Stage | Concrete deliverable | What would count as progress | What it would not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Close the present dynamics | Bound LF1's extended trace and the three-channel action error | A theorem covering a fixed critical interval, including threshold parameters and actual data | Natural action selection or experimental validity |
| 2. Independent numerical challenge | A separate PDE implementation, convergence in space/time/domain, energy and outgoing-flux budgets | Agreement with stated asymptotic orders across amplitudes and the w=4/3 neighbourhood, with uncertainty | A proof or independent peer review merely because tests pass |
| 3. Expose the effective mechanism | Dimensionless relationships among mode frequencies, energy transfer and memory | A predictive relation with a declared validity range and estimated truncation error | A photon or particle identification |
| 4. Build the FIN-to-action bridge | Explicit carrier, admissible dynamics, state/observable map and controlled limit | Derivation or sharply stated extra axioms, with locality, symmetry and conservation checked | Selection by naming the supplied PDE as its own explanation |
| 5. Establish a measurement map | Units, calibration, preparation and a specific observable in a specified physical system | Parameters fixed on one dataset and predictions tested on distinct data | Fundamental validation from a successful analogue experiment |
| 6. Test a distinctive prediction | One preregistered quantitative departure or constrained relation | Comparison with a baseline model, error budget and a defined rejection criterion | Confirmation by adjustable post-hoc fits |
| 7. Attempt broader reconstruction | Explicit extensions addressing dimensions, field content, quantum observables and gravitational coupling | Each bridge independently derived and confronted with constraints | An automatic route from a scalar soliton to the Standard Model or general relativity |

Stages 4 and 5 require information not provided by the present component
estimates. They should not be manufactured by inserting a new noise law,
clock, bath or microscopic update into the existing source contract.
The foundational bridge can be investigated alongside numerical work,
but remains a separately labelled hypothesis until established.

## The route I would prioritize

First complete a small, defensible result about energy transport and its
memory dependence. Then test it with an independently implemented solver.
A useful model-level prediction could be a dimensionless relation between
mode amplitudes and energy transfer, including a threshold-dependent
correction; its actual formula and error must still be derived. It cannot
be advertised as a measured law yet. An analogue system, if later selected
and justified, could test the mechanism, while leaving fundamental FIN
claims separate.

For the longer bridge I would use effective-field-theory reasoning:
identify relevant scales, observables, symmetry constraints and a controlled
matching/error scheme. This is a methodological recommendation, not proof
that the required scale hierarchy or limit exists for FIN. Background:
C. P. Burgess, *Introduction to Effective Field Theory*,
https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0701053 (2007). Its abstract describes exploiting
scale hierarchies; it contains no validation of this repository.

A credible endpoint must be able to fail: if the proposed FIN bridge cannot
produce the claimed symmetries or predictive relations without tuning to
the observations it is meant to explain, that candidate bridge should be
revised or rejected. Mathematical consistency, an analogue mechanism and
fundamental empirical success are three different achievements.
