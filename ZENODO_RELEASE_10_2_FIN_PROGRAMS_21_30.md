## Abstract

This release executes ten mathematical and computational research programs designed to test what can—and cannot—be inferred from the finite FIN strict kernel. It studies intervention-aware dynamical discrimination, positivity-preserving continuum refinements, graph-Dirac square roots, Osterwalder–Schrader reflection positivity, clock- and SPAM-robust identification, environment/dilation nonidentifiability, relational observables, adaptive integrability, executable no-go certificates, and a blinded synthetic held-out challenge.

The release also performs an independent inverse-action reconstruction. It tests the proposed interpretation

\[
K_{\mathrm{strict}}\approx a:\!(L+m^2I)^{-1}\!:
\]

as a contact-subtracted discrete screened Green operator and asks whether a smallest Lagrangian follows from it. The computation finds a strong but nonexact screened-resolvent approximation. Exact nearest-neighbour Yukawa identity is therefore refuted for the declared twelve-mode kernel. A conditional Gaussian action generates the un-subtracted covariance, while exact reconstruction of the zero-diagonal kernel requires a contact completion and is nonunique. The commonly proposed direct-sum action

\[
S[\phi,K]
=\frac12\phi^{T}(L+m^2I)\phi-J^{T}\phi
+\operatorname{tr}V(K)-\operatorname{tr}(KC)
\]

does not couple the field and kernel sectors unless an additional constraint or self-consistency law is supplied.

The report distinguishes exact theorems, numerical evidence, conjectures, and refutations throughout. Synthetic experiments are not represented as hardware validation or as evidence for fundamental physics.

## Main results

1. Two inequivalent positive refinements reproduce the finite strict anchors but converge to different continuum generators. The finite kernel does not select a unique continuum limit.
2. The cycle Laplacian admits an exact local incidence-Dirac square root with low-momentum exponent close to one. The strict weighted generator also admits an exact square root, but it is nonlocal.
3. A free massive temporal completion passes finite Osterwalder–Schrader tests, whereas positive higher-derivative quadratic completions can violate reflection positivity.
4. Restricted candidate dynamics can be separated under bounded clock and SPAM uncertainty, but unrestricted process tomography remains exponentially larger.
5. Population data do not identify the quantum dilation: two continuous quantum semigroups can agree on all population processes while differing strongly on coherent inputs.
6. Relational observables remove absolute frame labels, but orientation-odd quantities still require an explicit resource and do not generate a strict selector.
7. The tested adaptive covariance law has a strongly nonsymmetric Jacobian and therefore is not the gradient of a scalar potential in the declared coordinates.
8. Five finite no-go certificates pass reproducibly; a pinned proof-assistant port remains future work.
9. The blinded synthetic Program 30 challenge achieves 80% balanced classification accuracy under its preregistered finite protocol. This measures pipeline resolution only.
10. The strict kernel is approximated well, but not exactly, by a contact-subtracted nearest-neighbour screened resolvent. Exact inverse actions exist after contact completion, but form a nonunique generally nonlocal family.

## Scientific conclusion

Programs 21–30 strengthen the interpretation of FIN as a finite spectral and operational model class. They do not supply the structures still required for a physical theory: a justified temporal category, dimensional calibration, state-preparation and measurement instruments, an independently sourced adaptive law, selector data, and an empirical domain.

The inverse problem is therefore split into two mathematically different tasks. Recovering a quadratic precision from a positive covariance is standard and conditionally solvable. Recovering a unique physical law from a contact-subtracted finite kernel is underdetermined without independently motivated locality, contact, interaction, scale, clock, and observation axioms.
