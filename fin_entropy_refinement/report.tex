\begin{abstract}
The logarithmic mean represents finite graph heat flow as the gradient flow of
negative Shannon entropy, but the heat equation alone admits many
entropy--mobility pairs. We characterize what an additional binary-refinement
principle selects. In a common mass-coordinate, separable, edge-local class,
equal binary splitting permits every curvature
\(\phi''(x)=g(\log_2x)/x\) with positive continuous one-periodic \(g\).
An explicit smooth convex example disproves Shannon uniqueness under binary
splitting alone.

Adding concavity of the diagonal mobility---in particular, joint convexity
of the local flux cost---forces
\(\phi(x)=\kappa x\log x+\alpha x+\beta\).
Equal binary and ternary compatibility is an alternative sufficient
condition. Both are conditional theorems: metric compatibility is stronger
than the heat intertwiner already used in FIN.

For the Shannon mobility we prove a positive coarse Onsager defect. On a
connected graph with a strictly positive joint state, that defect vanishes
exactly for a product state. A second exact identity writes the missing
horizontal entropy dissipation as a weighted sum of conditional KL
divergences. Thus a coarse heat equation may close perfectly while concealing
fine correlations and dissipation. We exhibit this on the strict 12-to-24
FIN product graph.
\end{abstract}

The logarithmic-mean heat-gradient identity and its divided-difference
generalization are established results in finite Markov-chain transport.
Maas \cite{maas} gives this framework, including homogeneity/concavity
assumptions and the entropy-mobility relation. The results below are presented

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(W\one)-W.
\]
For strict FIN, \(n=12\) and
\[
W_{ij}=\frac{\cos(0.18575d+0.16250)}{1+d^{9/5}},\qquad
d=\min(|i-j|,12-|i-j|).

All relevant cosine arguments for \(d=1,\ldots,6\) lie in \((0,\pi/2)\).

\section{Heat flow does not by itself select entropy}

For \(\phi\in C^2(0,\infty)\), \(\phi''>0\), define
\[
F_\phi(p)=\sum_i\phi(p_i),\qquad
(K_M^W(p)\xi)_i=\sum_jW_{ij}M(p_i,p_j)(\xi_i-\xi_j).
\]
We require edgewise heat matching for every \(a,b>0\):
\begin{equation}
M(a,b)\big[\phi'(a)-\phi'(b)\big]=a-b. \label{match}
\end{equation}
This is a universal edge-local premise, not a conclusion from testing one
state on a fixed complete graph.

Equation \eqref{match} gives
\[
M_\phi(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{\phi'(a)-\phi'(b)},\qquad
M_\phi(a,a)=\frac1{\phi''(a)}.
\]
Then
\[
K_{M_\phi}^W(p)\nabla F_\phi(p)=Ap.
\]
For negative Shannon entropy, \(\phi(x)=x\log x\),
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{\log a-\log b}
=\int_0^1 a^t b^{1-t}\,dt,\qquad \Lambda(a,a)=a. \label{mean}
\end{equation}

The entropy dissipation is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_W(p)=
\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}(p_i-p_j)\log(p_i/p_j)\ge0. \label{diss}
\end{equation}
Along heat flow \(\dot p=-Ap\), negative Shannon \(F\) decreases and Shannon
\(S=-F\) increases. For boundary states, \(K_p\log p\) must be interpreted
through limiting edge fluxes; \(0\cdot\log0\) in the gradient expression is
not an ordinary finite product.

The identity represents a supplied heat generator. Microscopic rates are
already contained in \(W\); they are not inferred by constructing \(K_p\).
Without edge locality, a positive matrix annihilating both \(\one\) and
\(\log p\) can be added to \(K_p\), leaving the vector field unchanged.

\section{Refinement of masses and refinement of transport}

On a finite product graph,
\[
\widetilde A=A\otimes I_m+I_n\otimes L_B,\qquad
C=I_n\otimes\one_m^\top.
\]
C sums masses and is different from the Hilbert isometry
\(R=I_n\otimes\one_m/\sqrt m\). For a product state
\(P_{ib}=p_i r_b\), \(r_b>0\), \(\sum_b r_b=1\), impose
\begin{equation}
C K^{\widetilde W}_M(P)C^\top=K_M^W(p). \label{compat}
\end{equation}
Lifted potentials are constant in each fiber; vertical edges disappear from
the left side. Consequently the edgewise condition is
\begin{equation}
\sum_bM(r_ba,r_bb)=M(a,b). \label{split}
\end{equation}
Equal binary splitting gives
\begin{equation}
2M(a/2,b/2)=M(a,b). \label{binary}
\end{equation}

The repository's linear heat intertwiner \(C\widetilde A=AC\) holds for
every fine state and every vertical rate. It does not imply
\eqref{compat}. The latter preserves a quadratic form on \emph{all}
potentials, not just the entropy gradient used in the heat equation.

\section{A complete counterfamily for binary uniqueness}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic classification]
Under \eqref{match}, equal binary compatibility \eqref{binary} holds if and
only if
\[
\phi''(x)=\frac{g(\log_2x)}{x},
\]
where \(g\) is positive, continuous and one-periodic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take \(a=b=x\) in \eqref{binary}. The continuous diagonal formula implies
\(\phi''(x/2)=2\phi''(x)\). Thus \(x\phi''(x)\) is invariant under doubling.
Conversely, integrate this curvature from \(b/2\) to \(a/2\). A change of
variable gives
\(\phi'(a/2)-\phi'(b/2)=\phi'(a)-\phi'(b)\), which proves
\eqref{binary}.
\end{proof}

For \(\omega=2\pi/\log2\) and \(0<|\varepsilon|<1\), take
\[
\phi_\varepsilon(x)=x\log x-
\frac{\varepsilon x}{\omega(1+\omega^2)}
\big[\cos(\omega\log x)+\omega\sin(\omega\log x)\big].
\]
Then
\[
\phi_\varepsilon''(x)=
\frac{1+\varepsilon\sin(\omega\log x)}x>0.
\]
This is a smooth convex entropy on positive masses, continuous at zero.
Its mobility generates exactly the same heat flow, and it passes every
equal dyadic subdivision. It is not Shannon plus an affine function.

At \(\varepsilon=1/4\), \(a=0.07,b=0.19\), the binary relative defect is
\(2.22\times10^{-16}\), whereas ternary and \(0.3/0.7\) splitting defects are
\(0.09275176\) and \(0.08029200\).
Special pairs with dyadic ratios may conceal this difference. All numeric
figures are diagnostics for the analytic counterexample.

\section{Conditional entropy rigidity}

\begin{theorem}[Binary refinement and convex transport]
Assume \eqref{match}, \eqref{binary}, and that the diagonal mobility
\(f(x)=M(x,x)\) is concave. Then
\[
\boxed{
\phi(x)=\kappa x\log x+\alpha x+\beta,\qquad
M(a,b)=\Lambda(a,b)/\kappa,\quad\kappa>0.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The dyadic condition gives \(f(x/2)=f(x)/2\). The periodic classification and
positive continuity bound \(f(x)/x\), so \(f(0+)=0\).
For any concave \(f\) with \(f(0)=0\), \(f(x)/x\) is nonincreasing.
But this ratio takes equal values at \(x\) and \(2x\), hence is constant on
\([x,2x]\). Those intervals cover all positive scales. Write
\(f(x)=x/\kappa\); then \(\phi''(x)=\kappa/x\). Integrating proves the entropy
formula, and \eqref{match} determines the mobility.
\end{proof}

Diagonal concavity follows from joint convexity of the local transport cost
\[
\Psi(a,b,j)=\frac{j^2}{2M(a,b)}.
\]
To see this, restrict to \(a=b=x\), and at endpoints choose
\(j_i=f(x_i)\). At weight \(t\), put \(s=tf(x_1)+(1-t)f(x_2)>0\).
Convexity gives \(s^2/[2f(tx_1+(1-t)x_2)]\le s/2\), hence concavity of \(f\).
Conversely \(\Lambda\) is jointly concave by \eqref{mean}, and the convex
perspective \(j^2/(2z)\), decreasing in \(z>0\), proves convexity of the
Shannon transport cost.

This is a sufficient mathematical principle coupling refinement, information
and transport. The cost-convexity and full metric-compatibility premises are
additional assumptions for FIN. The result does not derive \(W\), its frozen
parameters or the time unit.

\begin{theorem}[Two independent subdivisions]
Heat matching plus continuous compatibility with equal binary and ternary
subdivision also forces the Shannon/logarithmic-mean pair up to scale.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\(h(x)=x\phi''(x)\) is invariant under multiplying by 2 and by 3.
On logarithmic coordinates the periods are \(\log2\) and \(\log3\).
Their ratio is irrational, since \(2^m=3^n\) has no positive integer
solution. Their integer span is dense; continuity makes \(h\) constant.
\end{proof}

Arbitrary binary split fractions similarly imply additive
\(s\mapsto M(sa,sb)\), hence degree-one homogeneity and Shannon. These
conditions are stronger than equal binary subdivision alone.

\subsection{Reference-measure caveat}

For a reversible reference \(\pi\), use density \(u_i=p_i/\pi_i\) and
relative entropy \(\sum_i\pi_i\phi(u_i)\).
If \(\pi\) is simultaneously refined to \(\pi\otimes r\), a product
state \(p\otimes r\) keeps density \(u_i\) unchanged in each child.
Any convex \(\phi\) can then inherit product mobility compatibility.
Quadratic relative entropy is an explicit non-Shannon example.
The preceding rigidity theorem is not invariant under arbitrarily changing
its mass/reference-measure conventions.

\section{Hidden correlation despite exact coarse dynamics}

Let \(P_{ib}>0\), \(p_i=\sum_bP_{ib}\), and \(r_i(b)=P_{ib}/p_i\).
For Shannon mobility define
\[
\mathcal D(P)=K_\Lambda^W(p)-
CK_\Lambda^{\widetilde W}(P)C^\top.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Coarse Onsager defect]
\(\mathcal D(P)\) is positive semidefinite. On a connected base graph it
vanishes if and only if \(P=p\otimes r\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Its edge conductance is \(W_{ij}\delta_{ij}\), where
\[
\delta_{ij}=\Lambda(p_i,p_j)-
\sum_b\Lambda(P_{ib},P_{jb})\ge0.
\]
Using the integral in \eqref{mean}, H\"older's inequality proves this bound.
For \(0<t<1\), equality holds exactly when the normalized child distributions
are equal: \(r_i=r_j\). Integrating retains the strict equality case.
Thus the defect is a positive Laplacian, zero exactly when equality holds
on every base edge. Connectedness gives independence globally.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hidden horizontal entropy production]
With \(\mathcal I_W\) as in \eqref{diss},
\[
\boxed{
\sum_b\mathcal I_W(P_{\cdot b})-\mathcal I_W(p)
=\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}
\big[p_i\KL(r_i\Vert r_j)+p_j\KL(r_j\Vert r_i)\big]\ge0.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On each edge split
\(\log(P_{ib}/P_{jb})=\log(p_i/p_j)+\log(r_i(b)/r_j(b))\).
The first part sums to the coarse dissipation; the two remaining weighted
terms are exactly the two KL divergences.
\end{proof}

The equality condition is the same independence criterion. Full fine
entropy production additionally contains the vertical contribution.
The defect is independent of that vertical rate. It requires fine-state
information and cannot be reconstructed from a coarse-only heat record.

\section{Physical meaning, limitations and next research}

The significant result for FIN is the coupling of entropy and mobility by
a precisely stated refinement/convexity principle, together with an exact
fine/coarse correlation diagnostic. The theorems are valid on any connected
positive weighted graph. They are not a fingerprint specific to the strict
parameter tuple.

The same \(A\) still supplies \(e^{-itA}\), \(e^{-tA}\) and
\(\cos(t\sqrt A)\). Unitary evolution preserves the density-matrix spectrum,
whereas the heat channel dissipates the convex entropy. Refinement rigidity
constrains the heat representation, not the measurement rule, the microscopic
configuration generator or an absolute clock.

Neither the new convexity axiom nor the stronger mobility equation has been
derived from the nadsoliton's historical coupling diagram. The factor
\(\kappa\), graph parameters, reference measure convention, physical preparation,
units, continuum tail and apparatus remain outside the conclusions.

The earlier abstract no-equivariant-section lemma is valid only when its
claimed vertical group preserves the admissible class. Arbitrary tail
translations do not generally preserve positivity or a physical action.
They must not be imported as physical symmetries without proof.

The following tasks have the highest decision value:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Derive or refute joint convexity of the transport cost from an independent
FIN action, without choosing it to obtain Shannon.
\item Determine whether an actual coarse measurement requires full Onsager
compatibility or only the weaker heat intertwiner.
\item Extend the correlation defect and its equality conditions through
multiple refinement levels.
\item Prove quantitative stability bounds for approximate dyadic compatibility
and approximate convexity.
\item Construct a physical preparation/measurement map only after the preceding
source premises are explicit.
\end{enumerate}

The log-periodic counterexample rules out automatic Shannon selection by
binary self-similarity. The conditional rigidity theorem explains one
sufficient extra principle. The KL identity identifies information hidden
by a coarse layer. None establishes a physical Theory of Everything.
