# Primary-literature database for the post-SRN FIN research decision

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: annotated external literature, not an accepted FIN
claim register. Fourteen readable primary PDF works and one2026 primary HTML
article are registered in
[LITERATURE_REGISTER.json](LITERATURE_REGISTER.json). An unread original scan
is recorded separately. An annotation describes the portions inspected, not
a claim that every proof in a long paper was checked.

The immediate recommendation is **native interacting temporal transfer
theory**: derive the relevant operator/Dirichlet-form limit from the actual
finite-parent FIN dynamics. A general stochastic-to-Hilbert-space map is
already known and is not the proposed discovery. The unpaid work is the
nonlinear critical, interacting, finite-parent limit and its observational
meaning. Strong convergence, compact convergence and physical quantum
interpretation are separate gates.

Two tempting locality routes have failed the present model-specific tests:
the proposed scalar Bernstein representation and the natural-order circular-
planar passive realization. Those are restricted failures, not a proof that
FIN can never support locality. The circular-planar question was already
REF-004 in the historical task catalog: the present explicit minor restores
checkable evidence for an old question, rather than creating a new campaign.
The actual FIN certificates belong to the accompanying research analysis,
not to any of the papers below.

## Reading and provenance rules

- PDFs were read through Firecrawl text extraction; the SHA-256 values bind
  the actual extracted text, not the original PDF bytes or a witnessed
  historical download. Extraction occasionally mangles formulas; theorem
  labels, hypotheses and surrounding derivations were checked together.
- Full copyrighted PDFs are not copied into the tracked research package.
  The ignored `.firecrawl/fin_toe_20261015/` cache is a local reading aid.
  Its absence in a clean checkout must be reported as an absent cache, not a
  missing FIN proof dependency or reconstructed source hash.
- ArXiv identifiers, explicit revision labels and publisher metadata are
  distinguished from a TeX-generated date. In particular, Caffarelli--
  Silvestre is a 2006/2007 work, **not a new 2024 result** merely because the
  extracted title page prints a later date.
- The reading includes a2024 Mosco revision and a2026 primary metastability
  article. It is not an exhaustive review through October2026. Firecrawl
  credits were exhausted; after explicit user permission, local
  browser-harness accessed the official2026 article and its theorem sections.
  No paid cloud browser, credit purchase or plan upgrade was used.

## A. Native temporal/operator bridge: the recommended tools

### LIT-001 — Kuwae and Shioya (2003)

[Convergence of spectral structures: a functional analytic theory and its
applications to spectral geometry](https://www.intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1805786134762446850-1805786134762446850-e776617e92e2a310c0026f43fb47be64.pdf).

Read: varying-Hilbert-space framework, Definition 2.11, Theorems 2.4 and 2.6.
Mosco convergence is equivalent to the appropriate strong semigroup and
resolvent convergence; spectral counting requires the stronger compact
convergence. FIN must supply its changing measures, identification maps,
liminf/recovery arguments and, when needed, compactness. A diffusion path
limit alone does not pay these hypotheses. Useful framework, not a ready-made
FIN spectral theorem or emergent spatial geometry.

### LIT-002 — Henley (2003 preprint; 2004 publication)

[From classical to quantum dynamics at Rokhsar--Kivelson points](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0311345).

Read: general mapping, Section 3.4 and Section 4. Detailed balance supplies
a symmetric similarity transform; the ground vector has components
proportional to square roots of stationary probabilities. Diagonal classical
correlations match the constructed Hamiltonian's Euclidean correlations.
FIN can reuse this identity, but an imaginary-time representation does not
select physical real-time evolution, noncommuting instruments or Born-rule
experiments. The proposed new work is native interacting convergence, not
rediscovery of this generic construction.

### LIT-003 — Grothaus and Wittmann (2021 preprint; v2, 2024)

[Mosco convergence of gradient forms with non-convex interaction potential](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05140).

Read: introduction, Theorem 3.4, Conditions 3.6/3.8 and Theorem 3.11. This
extends available convergence methods beyond log-concave reference measures.
Closability, conditional-density controls and a domain condition remain
essential. FIN's parent is a discrete jump form, not this paper's gradient
form; a jump-to-gradient comparison is unpaid. The paper motivates a proof
route but cannot be quoted as automatically proving the critical FIN limit.

## B. Quantum-interpretation kill tests, not automatic reconstruction

### LIT-004 — Abramsky and Brandenburger (2011, v7)

[The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and Contextuality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.0264).

Read: measurement covers, Sections 3--4, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 8.1 with
its construction. A compatible empirical model has a factorizable hidden-
variable realization exactly when it has a probability global section.
Passive compatible readouts of one FIN history already admit such a joint
law. This blocks contextuality claims from classical re-description alone;
it does not settle invasive or contextual protocols whose operational
identifications have not been specified.

### LIT-005 — Spekkens (2004 preprint; 2005 publication)

[Contextuality for preparations, transformations and unsharp measurements](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0406166).

Read: Section II's operational equivalences, equations (1)--(7), and the
convex-preparation discussion. Generalized noncontextuality concerns equal
representations of operationally equivalent procedures, not merely whether a
model uses classical variables. FIN must export an explicit preparation/
transformation/readout operational theory before this test applies. A
restricted classical experiment is not automatically universally
noncontextual; neither does memory or measurement disturbance establish
quantum contextuality.

## C. Exact locality representations: applicability must be tested first

### LIT-006 — Kwaśnicki and Mucha (2017 preprint)

[Extension technique for complete Bernstein functions of the Laplace operator](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.02475).

Read: scalar extension setup, complete Bernstein representation and Theorem
3.1's Krein-string correspondence, with its boundary qualifications. Positive
strings correspond to complete Bernstein functions of a supplied base
Laplacian. The local extension is not available for every positive spectral
matrix. The present FIN scalar candidate fails a necessary concavity test;
choosing a different base operator or a vector extension would be a different
bridge requiring fresh premises and tests.

### LIT-007 — Caffarelli and Silvestre (2006 preprint; v2, 2007)

[An extension problem related to the fractional Laplacian](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0608640).

Read: Section 1 and Sections 3.1--3.2's Poisson/Fourier-energy argument. The
fractional power with exponent in (0,1) is a weighted local extension's
boundary operator, with the appropriate normalization and function class.
It presupposes the base Euclidean Laplacian and is not an inverse theorem
producing physical space from an arbitrary FIN kernel. Previously established
fractional FIN asymptotics must not be counted as a new discovery here.

### LIT-008 — Alman, Lian and Tran (2013 preprint; revision unbound)

[Circular Planar Electrical Networks I: The Electrical Poset EP_n](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.2697).

Read: Sections 2.1--2.3, Definitions 2.2.1/2.2.4 and Theorems 2.2.6,
2.3.6/2.3.8. The exact sign/order convention turns circular minors into a
necessary-and-sufficient passive disk-network criterion. Conductances are
uniquely recoverable only after fixing a critical graph; Y--Delta freedom
prevents unrestricted geometric uniqueness. The restored FIN minor fails
in the natural port order. This neither tests every order nor excludes all
nonplanar or nonpassive localization mechanisms.

### LIT-009 — Kenyon and Wilson (2006 preprint)

[Boundary Partitions in Trees and Dimers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0608422).

Read: Sections 1.1--1.2, Theorem 1.1's scope and Appendix A. Boundary response
and grove connection weights are related by explicit polynomial identities;
the standard positive Dirichlet-to-Neumann matrix is a Schur complement.
These are precise consistency tools for a supplied planar network. They do
not create the network, uniquely select its geometry, or turn classical
Gaussian correlations into quantum matter. Its response-entry sign convention
must not be confused with the FIN Laplacian notation.

### LIT-010 — Dörfler and Bullo (2011 preprint)

[Kron Reduction of Graphs With Applications to Electrical Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2950).

Read: Lemma 2.1 with proof and Theorem 3.4. Schur reduction preserves the
passive Laplacian/M-matrix class and can make a sparse interior network's
boundary operator dense. Thus dense boundary response alone is not a
locality no-go, while static response alone does not identify hidden topology
or dynamic storage. FIN already used this algebra; the useful new assessment
is whether a specific, more restrictive realization class actually fits.

## D. Composition/continuum benchmarks: do not insert their laws into FIN

### LIT-011 — Caron and Fox (2014 preprint; 2017 publication)

[Sparse graphs using exchangeable random measures](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.1137).

Read: Section 2.1/Theorem 1 and Section 4.2/Theorems 5--7. Dense-or-empty
applies to one infinite jointly exchangeable adjacency array with its
projective restrictions, not every finite permutation-invariant family.
Sparse measure models change the sampled object and impose CRM/Lévy
conditions. This clarifies the contract for a future FIN incidence theorem;
it does not derive a sparse graph from exchangeable FIN copies. Importing
the CRM would introduce a premise.

### LIT-012 — Borgs, Chayes, Cohn and Holden (2018 publisher PDF)

[Sparse Exchangeable Graphs and Their Limits via Graphon Processes](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume18/16-421/16-421.pdf).

Read: Sections 2.1--2.4 and Theorems 15/26/27/28. Sigma-finite graphons,
tail regularity and a specified sampling operation give an appropriate sparse
limit; identification is only up to cut-distance equivalence. These results
do not imply metric-manifold convergence or finite-speed propagation. FIN's
existing graphon/nonlocality result is reused, not reopened. A new incidence
object and its genuine sampling/refinement rule would have to exist first.

### LIT-013 — Konopka, Markopoulou and Severini (2008)

[Quantum graphity: A model of emergent locality](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/2472826/e104029.pdf).

Read: Section II.C, Section III.A and Section VI. The model supplies edge
degrees of freedom, a preferred-valence term and cycle-sensitive interactions;
candidate low-energy graph comparisons illustrate both opportunities and
assumption costs. Sparse regular graphs need not have finite-dimensional
spatial scaling. This is a falsification benchmark, not permission to add
edges, cycle rewards or a clock and call them derived FIN. It supplies no
independent A7 source.

## E. Recent primary benchmark inspected with browser-harness

### LIT-014 — Kim and Lee (2026, v1)

[Sharp mixing time asymptotics of Glauber dynamics for the Curie-Weiss-Potts
model at low temperatures](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19545v1).

Read in official HTML: Theorem1.4, Assumption3.1, Theorem3.3 and
Theorems3.7/3.8; metadata confirms submission on23 February2026. The result
separates metastable trace reduction, local mixing and recurrence before
obtaining sharp global mixing. It assumes the specified Potts law and a
fixed low-temperature regime, with exclusions stated in Theorem1.4. FIN's
A7 interaction and critical tuning do not meet those premises by name
alone. This is a current scope benchmark, not a source for FIN's unpaid
global B4/prefactor claim. Only HTML was read for this record, not its PDF.

### LIT-015 — Jaffe and Ritter (2006 author preprint)

[Quantum Field Theory on Curved Backgrounds via the Euclidean Functional
Integral](https://www.arthurjaffe.com/Assets/documents/CurvedSpace.pdf).

Read: Section1.8, Definition1.5/Theorem1.11, Sections2.1/2.5/2.6 and
Theorems2.7–2.9. The paper separates reflection positivity, Hilbert quotient,
transfer semigroup and subsequent Schrödinger interpretation. Its spatial
construction assumes a suitable supplied geometry and reflection/static
structure. FIN's actual Markov history provides temporal positivity only;
this paper does not license assigning it a spatial or gravitational meaning.
It is a benchmark for the bridge prerequisites, not an applied FIN theorem.

## Unread original and acquisition limits

[Curtis, Ingerman and Morrow, Circular planar graphs and resistor
networks](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~curtis/cim.pdf) (1998) was fetched
as a scanned PDF, but extraction yielded mostly image placeholders and
isolated formulas. The original proof was **not read**. The readable precise
characterization was checked in LIT-008; sign and Schur conventions were
also checked in LIT-009. Do not cite access to the scan as verification of
the original proof. Its failed-text hash is retained as acquisition metadata.

The wider cache includes exploratory hits not admitted into this thirteen-
paper toolset. Search-result descriptions and abstracts are discovery aids,
not verified theorem applications. No claim of comprehensive 2026 coverage,
new natural source, quantum/gravity derivation or empirical validation is
exported by this bibliography.
