\begin{abstract}
For a projectively consistent, exchangeable family of finite-state Markov
population generators with fixed singleton generator $Q$, let $b_{ij}$ be
the instantaneous rate at which both coordinates change when initially
prepared at $(i,j)$. Arbitrarily large replication forces the matrix
$B=(b_{ij})$ to be a completely positive real matrix, hence positive
semidefinite. Consequently $b_{ii}=0$ for every origin forces $B=0$ and
the independent tensor-sum generator at every population size. Small
diagonal activities give linear, rather than merely square-root,
population-error bounds under these premises.

A strict-FIN construction is consistent, exchangeable, reversible and
dihedrally symmetric through population size three, with zero same-origin
pair activity and positive mixed-origin activity. It cannot be extended
consistently to size four. Thus the unlimited-replication premise is
essential. Conversely, even maximal possible collective activity leaves
different irreducible microscopic laws with the same singleton dynamics,
equilibrium, symmetries and activity matrix.

The historical coupling diagrams and mirror operators do not supply the
required population-composition law. An exact interval/integer audit also
finds two fixed points and 111 two-cycles for a declared synchronous
threshold network with strict weights. The canonical legacy network has
six fixed points and 64 two-cycles. These correct an unsupported unique
attractor interpretation; they are not physical vacua or solitons.
No microscopic population, unit, selector, apparatus or ToE closure is
derived. The next target is a sourced, transition-resolved composition law.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite replication: a rate-counting theorem}

Let $G_N$ be finite-state continuous-time Markov generators on $X^N$.
Assume exchangeability under coordinate permutations and strong projective
consistency under deleting any coordinates. In particular every singleton
has the same generator $Q$ at every configuration, not only at equilibrium.
Write $r_i=-Q_{ii}<\infty$. For two coordinates initially at $(i,j)$ define
\begin{align}
 b_{ij}&=\sum_{a\ne i,\,b\ne j}
             G_2((i,j),(a,b)),\qquad \delta_i=b_{ii},\\
 \beta_{ij}^{\,b}&=\sum_{a\ne i}G_2((i,j),(a,b)),\qquad b\ne j.
\end{align}
Exchangeability gives $b_{ij}=b_{ji}$. The diagonal $b_{ii}$ concerns two
different coordinates that share an initial vertex; it is \emph{not} a
single-coordinate self-loop $Q_{ii}$ or $W_{ii}$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite same-origin bound]
If the family extends consistently through $m+1$ coordinates and
$\delta_i=0$, then
\[
 b_{ij}\le\frac{r_j}{m},\qquad
 \beta_{ij}^{\,b}\le\frac{Q_{jb}}{m}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Prepare $m$ coordinates at $i$ and one at $j$. Strong consistency makes
the joint-change rate of any pair among the $i$ coordinates equal to
$\delta_i=0$. Positivity therefore excludes every event changing two or
more of these $m$ coordinates. Let $Z$ count changed $i$ coordinates in an
event, and $Y$ indicate whether the $j$ coordinate changes. Thus $ZY\le Y$.
Summing over positive event rates gives
$m b_{ij}\le r_j$. Replacing $Y$ by the indicator of the specific
transition $j\to b$ proves the target-resolved bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Nonzero same-origin activity]
For $r_j>0$ and $\delta_i\ge0$, the same construction gives
\[
 b_{ij}\le F_m(r_j,\delta_i)
 :=\frac{r_j}{2m}\left(1+
 \sqrt{1+\frac{4m(m-1)\delta_i}{r_j}}\right).
\]
If $r_j=0$, then $b_{ij}=0$ directly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\langle\cdot\rangle_g$ mean summation over the event rates in that
prepared configuration. Consistency gives
\[
 \langle ZY\rangle_g=m b_{ij},\quad
 \langle Y\rangle_g=r_j,\quad
 \langle Z(Z-1)\rangle_g=m(m-1)\delta_i.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz in the positive measure restricted to $Y=1$ gives
$(m b_{ij})^2\le r_j\langle Z^2Y\rangle_g$.
Also $\langle Z^2Y\rangle_g\le m(m-1)\delta_i+m b_{ij}$.
Solving the resulting quadratic inequality proves the bound.
\end{proof}

For zero $\delta_i$ this reduces to $r_j/m$. For positive $\delta_i$
its limit is $\sqrt{r_j\delta_i}$. The next theorem improves that
one-group estimate by replicating \emph{both} origins, or all origins
together.

\section{The activity-Gram theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Replication activity matrix]
Let $B=(b_{ij})$ and let $n=|X|$. If a consistent family exists through
$nm$ coordinates, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 B+\frac1m\diag(r_i-\delta_i)
 \quad\text{is a completely positive real matrix}.
\end{equation}
If it extends to arbitrarily large population sizes, $B$ itself is a
completely positive real matrix. In particular,
\[
 B\succeq0,\qquad 0\le b_{ij}\le\sqrt{\delta_i\delta_j}.
\]
Here ``completely positive matrix'' means $ZZ^T$ for an entrywise
nonnegative real matrix $Z$; it is not terminology for a quantum channel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Prepare $m$ coordinates at each origin $i$. For one event let $Z_i$ count
the changed coordinates in group $i$. These are nonnegative integers.
The matrix
\[
 M_m=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{y\ne x}g(x,y)Z(x,y)Z(x,y)^T
\]
is a sum of nonnegative rank-one outer products. Projective consistency
gives
\[
 \langle Z_i\rangle_g=mr_i,\quad
 \langle Z_i(Z_i-1)\rangle_g=m(m-1)\delta_i,\quad
 \langle Z_iZ_j\rangle_g=m^2b_{ij}\ (i\ne j).
\]
These identities yield exactly \eqref{eq:gram}. Taking arbitrarily large
$m$ gives the limit $B$.

For completeness, the real completely-positive cone is closed here.
In the finite-dimensional space of symmetric matrices, any conic
combination can be reduced by linear dependence to at most $n(n+1)/2$
nonnegative rank-one terms. Normalize each nonzero generating vector to
unit norm. The coefficients then sum to the matrix trace. Along a convergent
sequence the coefficients are bounded, the vectors lie in a compact
nonnegative part of the unit sphere, and a subsequence of these finite
representations converges to one of the same form. Thus $B$ has such a
representation. Its $2\times2$ principal minors give
$b_{ij}^2\le\delta_i\delta_j$.
\end{proof}

For just two origins, $2m$ copies suffice for the corresponding principal
matrix. Hence a finite family through $M$ copies gives
\[
 b_{ij}\le
 \sqrt{\left(\delta_i+\frac{r_i-\delta_i}{m}\right)
             \left(\delta_j+\frac{r_j-\delta_j}{m}\right)},
 \qquad m=\lfloor M/2\rfloor.
\]
This can be combined with the one-group bound rather than replacing it:
the latter can be sharper at small horizons or for a specified target.
For equal $r_i=s$ and $\delta_i\le\delta\le s$, the two-group bound is
$\delta+(s-\delta)/m$, and the infinite-horizon bound is simply $\delta$.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional independence from same-origin tests]
For an exchangeable strongly projectively consistent family at arbitrarily
large sizes, $b_{ii}=0$ for every $i$ forces $G_N=\sum_{k=1}^NQ^{(k)}$
for every finite $N$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The Gram bound forces every $b_{ij}$ to vanish. In any $N$-coordinate
configuration, strong consistency makes the pair-change rate for each
coordinate pair equal to its corresponding $b_{ij}$. Every event changing
two or more coordinates contributes positively to some pair rate and is
therefore absent. Only single-coordinate jumps remain; autonomous
singleton rates fix each of their rates to $Q$. Row sums fix the diagonal.
\end{proof}

This reduces the \emph{conditional} activity test to $|X|$ same-origin
preparations, but it imports an unlimited-replication premise. No finite
experiment verifies that premise for every size. A FIN derivation must
establish the composition rule mathematically. The theorem does not say
that independence is physically preferable, and a valid FIN law might
instead predict nonzero joint activity.

\subsection{Quantitative propagation to the previous theorem}

\]
Consistency gives
$\mathcal B_N(x)=\sum_{k<l}b_{x_kx_l}$. Under unlimited replication and
$\max_i\delta_i\le\delta$,
\[
 \sup_x\mathcal B_N(x)\le\binom N2\delta.
\]
The preceding campaign's finite-generator perturbation argument therefore

\[
 \sup_x\TV\left(e^{tG_N}(x,\cdot),
                     e^{t\sum_kQ^{(k)}}(x,\cdot)\right)

\]

$k$ coordinates by its $k$ marginal single events. The row total-variation

\section{A sharp finite-horizon strict-FIN counterexample}

Let $X=C_{12}$, $f(i)=i+6\pmod{12}$, and let the two parity classes be
even and odd vertices. The antipodal map preserves parity. Put
$a=W_{i,f(i)}=W_6$ and fix a proposed maximum population size $M$ and
\[
 c=\frac{a}{M-1}.
\]
For each $N\le M$ use the following rates.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Coordinate $k$ makes each non-antipodal transition with its original
rate $W_{x_kj}$.
\item Its antipodal rate is $a-c n_k^{\rm opp}$, where
$n_k^{\rm opp}$ is the number of other coordinates in the opposite parity
class.
\item Each unordered pair of coordinates in opposite parity classes
makes the simultaneous antipodal move at rate $c$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Finite consistency without infinite extendibility]
This defines a nonnegative, exchangeable, strongly projectively consistent
family through size $M$. Its singleton generator is exactly strict $Q$.
It is reversible with the same product-uniform equilibrium and has the
full $D_{12}$ symmetry of the supplied graph. It is irreducible. Its pair
activities satisfy
\[
 b_{ij}=\begin{cases}c,&i,j\text{ have opposite parity},\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad b_{ii}=0.
\]
For $c>0$ fixed as above, no family satisfying the same premises and the
same two-coordinate generator can extend through size $M+1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity follows from $n_k^{\rm opp}\le N-1\le M-1$.
The total antipodal rate of a coordinate is
$a-c n_k^{\rm opp}+c n_k^{\rm opp}=a$. Deleting a partner converts its
joint event, if active, into an observed singleton antipodal event; this
exactly compensates the deleted partner's subtraction from the singleton
rate. All other rates restrict unchanged. This proves strong consistency,
not just agreement of a numerical marginal.

An antipodal move preserves all parity labels. Its reverse therefore has
the same partner counts and rate. Each common antipodal map is an
involution. Non-antipodal rates are symmetric, so the full generator is
symmetric and product-uniform measure is reversible. Non-antipodal
single-coordinate edges include the connected nearest-neighbour cycle and
remain positive; hence all configurations communicate. Particle permutations
preserve the construction. Vertex rotations and reflections commute with
the antipode and preserve the relation ``opposite parity'', even when they
exchange the two parity classes; hence there is no selected parity label or
orientation.

For origins $(0,1)$ the target-resolved joint rate for the second coordinate
to make $1\to7$ is $c=a/(M-1)$. If an extension to $M+1$ existed, the
finite same-origin theorem with $m=M$ would instead require $c\le a/M$.
This is a strict contradiction. It excludes every extension with the stated
premises, not merely the displayed formula beyond its domain.
\end{proof}

For $M=3$,
\[
 a\simeq0.01107081732144,\quad c\simeq0.00553540866072.
\]
The implemented $G_2,G_3$ have 144 and 1728 states. The deletion residual
is about $2.66\times10^{-15}$, singleton residual $8.88\times10^{-16}$,
and reversibility residual zero. All 24 dihedral transformations were
checked. The forbidden fourth-copy target bound is $a/3<c$.
The algebraic proof, not the decimal residuals, licenses these conclusions.

The activity matrix has zero diagonal and positive opposite-parity blocks.
For $v_i=(-1)^i$,
\[
 v^TBv=-72c<0.
\]
It therefore also violates the necessary infinite-replication Gram
condition. A finite consistency check, even together with equilibrium and
symmetry, must not be silently promoted to unlimited self-similarity.

\section{A new useful object, but no unique ``maximum resonance'' law}

The common-vertex-transposition model from the previous campaign has
activity matrix
\[
 B_\theta=\theta(sI+W),\qquad 0\le\theta\le1.
\]
Indeed, two coordinates at the same origin $i$ leave together whenever a
common transposition touches $i$, giving diagonal rate $\theta s$.
Two different origins change together only when their connecting pair is
transposed, giving $\theta W_{ij}$.

This is the signless Laplacian, with an explicit nonnegative incidence
factorization:
\[
 sI+W=\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T.
\]
By comparison,
\[
 A=sI-W=\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T.
\]
The same edge data therefore admit an oriented difference form for mean
diffusion and an unsigned Gram form for one collective-event realization.
This is standard graph algebra with an explicit FIN application, not a
new physical duality theorem. The event construction is what gives the
plus form its activity interpretation, and $\theta$ is still free.
In particular, $W_{ii}=0$ does \emph{not} imply $b_{ii}=0$.

One might try to select the missing dynamics by maximizing collective
activity, as a precise proxy for the informal ``maximum resonance'' idea.
Even that proxy does not determine the microscopic process.

\begin{theorem}[Maximal-activity ambiguity]
There are distinct irreducible, reversible, exchangeable, strongly
projectively consistent families with singleton generator $Q$, all strict
graph symmetries, the same product-uniform equilibrium, and the same
entrywise maximal activity matrix $B=s\one\one^T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $R=W/s$, which is symmetric, stochastic and zero on the diagonal.
Define
\[
 G_N^{\rm prod}=s(R^{\otimes N}-I),\qquad
 (G_N^{\rm shift}h)(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{11}W_{0r}
                     [h(x+r\one)-h(x)].
\]
The first model has one rate-$s$ Poisson clock, followed by conditionally
independent vertex transitions at that tick. The second has common cyclic
translations. Every coordinate changes at every event in either model,
so every $b_{ij}=s$. This is the maximum possible rate since a pair-change
event is contained in either coordinate's rate-$s$ departure event.

Both generators restrict to $Q$ on one coordinate and restrict consistently
under deletion. They are exchangeable. Symmetry of $R$ and the equality of
opposite-shift rates give reversibility, while circulant structure gives
the stated graph symmetries. For each $0\le\alpha<1$ the mixture
$(1-\alpha)G_N^{\rm prod}+\alpha G_N^{\rm shift}$ is irreducible:
$R^2$ is entrywise positive on this complete graph, so two product ticks
can reach every configuration. All these mixtures retain the same $B$.

From $(0,0)$, however, the product model has transition rate
$W_{01}W_{02}/s>0$ to $(1,2)$, while a common shift has zero rate for that
transition. Thus different mixtures have different generators.
\end{proof}

For mixture weights $\alpha=0.25,0.75$, the strict target rates are about
$0.04077154$ and $0.01359051$, although the entire activity matrix is
identical. Hence the observed number of simultaneous changes can still
omit \emph{which} changes occur. A future source law must address the
transition-resolved object
\[
 T_{(i,a),(j,b)}=G_2((i,j),(a,b)),\qquad a\ne i,\ b\ne j,
\]
and, potentially, higher-order composition. Its further classification is
recommended work, not a result claimed in this campaign.

\section{Retained falsification: an invisible finite-time exception}

An attempted regression test expected every mixed-event model in the
finite construction to differ from independence in endpoint distributions
after pure same-origin preparation. This expectation failed for a uniform
four-state kernel. The failure was investigated, not erased by weakening
the numerical tolerance.

Take $X=C_4$, $W_{ij}=1$ off the diagonal, antipode $i+2$, and the finite
construction above. The singleton solution from origin $i$ is
\[
 p_t=u+e^{-4t}(e_i-u).
\]
The parity class not containing $i$ has equal probabilities at its two
vertices. In the opposite-parity block, the correction to the independent
forward generator applied to $p\otimes p$ is proportional to
\[
 [p(f(a))-p(a)]\,[p(f(b))-p(b)].
\]
One factor vanishes. Outside that block the correction is zero. Thus
$p_t\otimes p_t$ solves both forward equations from every pure clone
preparation at every time. The uniqueness of the finite linear initial-value
problem proves exact endpoint equality. Mixed-origin preparations instead
distinguish the processes. This is not equality of all conditional paths or
event records.

The strict weights break this special within-class balance. For strict
preparation $(0,0)$ at time $0.4$, the numerically computed endpoint TV
difference is about $0.0006491751$, despite zero joint-change rate at the
initial clone configuration. Both the uniform exception and the strict
distinction remain in the test suite.

\subsection{A general obstruction to one signed-spin fixed point}

For any real symmetric zero-diagonal $W$, the finite energy
$E(x)=-x^TWx/2$ has a minimizer. Flipping spin $i$ changes the energy by
$2x_i(Wx)_i$. At a global minimum this is nonnegative, so that state is a
fixed point of the threshold rule with the stated tie convention.
Since $E(-x)=E(x)$, its distinct negative is also a fixed point. A unique
fixed state is therefore impossible in this unquotiented model. For strict
positive connected weights, the only ground configurations are the two
uniform spin signs. Introducing a sign quotient or bias would be a new
model choice, not something established by the old script.

\subsection{Update schedules and excitation/inhibition are separate data}

A single asynchronous alignment update never increases this energy:
if a spin flips against its local field, $2x_i(Wx)_i\le0$. A simultaneous
update need not decrease it, and the certified two-cycles already refute
global convergence to one fixed point. Thus a single-coordinate update
schedule builds absence of simultaneous changes into the algorithm; it
does not derive that absence from the weight matrix. The original Hopfield
paper likewise specifies an evolution algorithm rather than identifying
arbitrary static matrices with complete dynamics \cite{hopfield}.

Finally, an excitation/inhibition weight-sum ratio cannot determine linear
stability. For the supplied continuous extension
\[
 \dot z=-z+gVz,
\]
the largest growth eigenvalue is $-1+g\lambda_{\max}(V)$.
For any nonzero symmetric zero-diagonal $V$, trace zero implies
$\lambda_{\max}(V)>0$. Choosing
$g=1/(2\lambda_{\max})$ or $2/\lambda_{\max}$ gives maximum growth
$-1/2$ or $+1$, with exactly the same matrix and E/I ratio. The canonical
legacy line ratio is approximately $0.59904$ in both cases. A leak,
activation slope and gain must be specified before ``critical'' or
``stable'' has a defined dynamical meaning. This does not transfer a
linear-flow result to the binary threshold dynamics or to physical matter.

\section{Dual dynamics and the remaining source obligations}

They remain different functions of one self-adjoint operator, with
different operational semantics. The new activity matrices belong to a
declared classical population extension; they do not equate heat with
Born probabilities, derive a bath, or select a coherent quantum completion.

The constructive advance is conditional: unlimited exchangeable replication
forces an activity Gram structure, and zero diagonal then forces
independence. Its premises are not yet strict-sourced. Finite replication
does not suffice, and maximizing activity does not uniquely select the
alternative. We therefore neither impose independence nor reject
self-coupling: the actual theory must determine its activity and
transition laws.

Known exchangeable-process results permit common stochastic influences

general. The proofs here are elementary finite-generator arguments and do
not rely on an unverified application of a general representation theorem.
No claim is made that these general mathematical mechanisms are newly
discovered in the literature. Their role is to sharpen FIN's source tests.
